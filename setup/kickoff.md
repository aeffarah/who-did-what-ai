# Kickoff

Three opening moves, taken from the [take-home example](../example/take-home/session_log.md). They take about five minutes and set up the rest of the session.

## 1. Scope check

Confirm the AI is working where you think it is before it does anything.

> are you linked to skill check

**Why:** an assistant acts on whatever it can see. Making sure it's in the right folder costs a few seconds, and starting in the wrong place costs much more.

## 2. Read-only context load

Give it the source material and tell it explicitly not to act yet.

> added a pdf into skill check, I've started a timer, don't do anything yet just read this new pdf for context then we can go from there

**Why:** it separates understanding from doing. You get to see how the AI reads the task before it commits to a direction, which is also your first chance to check its interpretation against yours.

## 3. Give it T0 and your constraints

Give it your stopwatch start time, and tell it what the output has to reflect about you. In live mode the AI starts the log on its own; after the fact, the transcript picks up both.

> I want to keep track of things such as my prompt engineering, where my brain went vs what you contributed [...] And I want it to honestly reflect my strengths and weaknesses and how I work.

**Why:** constraints stated up front become the yardstick for the whole session. Without them, the AI's defaults fill the gap, and correcting that later is what fills a log with ⚠ entries.

## During the session

Two moves that paid off repeatedly in the example:

- **Pause before committing.** When the AI says "confirm and I'll proceed," ask what's missing first.
- **Re-read the source.** Check the output against the original ask. In the example this caught something three times, and the AI never did it without being asked.
