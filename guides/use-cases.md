# Use cases

The log format stays the same. What changes is what the WORK layer covers, who reads the retrospective, and what to watch for.

| Use case | WORK layer | Retrospective reader | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Assessments and take-homes** | The exercise | The reviewer, often as a "how did you use AI" section | Keep it to one page and keep the log local. A six-page audit trail fails the exercise on its own. |
| **Project and design work** | The spec, decision or analysis | Your team or stakeholders | AI-supplied facts that slip into the doc unverified |
| **Debugging** | Hypotheses, checks, what was ruled out | Whoever picks it up next | The ruled-out list is a hand-off packet; it saves the next person repeating your checks |
| **Learning a new domain** | Concepts and claims | You | Plausible-sounding claims you can't yet judge. Tag every one to verify |
| **Writing** | The draft | You or an editor | Form corrections will dominate. Check whether the ideas are still yours after the rounds |
| **Coding (untested)** | Design calls, rejected approaches, conventions enforced | Your team, often as a PR note | Overhead in long agentic sessions. See [Status](../README.md#status-mvp) and tell us how it goes |
| **Portfolio evidence** | Whatever you built | Hiring managers, peers | A strengths-only log reads as marketing. The weaknesses are what make it credible |

## Picking a size

Not every session needs every section. The instructions default to Standard; to use another size, tell the AI at the start.

- **Light:** timeline and course-corrections only. Good for short sessions or debugging.
- **Standard:** the full template. Good for anything you'll show someone.
- **Retrospective only:** write the one-pager from memory right after the session. It's the weakest version, and still better than nothing.
